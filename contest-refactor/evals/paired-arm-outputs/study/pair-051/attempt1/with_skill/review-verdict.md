## What I'm not clearing at 9.5

**The evidence proves less than the claim.** The Actor's report says the change made the cache "safe to share across the prefetch tasks." The diff's own trailing note is the load-bearing fact here: two distinct entry points, `prefetch` and `warmThumbnails`, both reach `insert` concurrently, called from the same screen appearance. `parallelInsertsAreSerialized` hammers `insert` with 64 homogeneous concurrent tasks — that proves the dictionary itself no longer corrupts or crashes under concurrent write, which is real and worth crediting. It does **not** exercise the actual named scenario: two *different-purpose* callers (a full prefetch path and a thumbnail-warm path) writing concurrently, possibly under the *same key* with *different content* (thumbnail vs. full-resolution image). Nothing in the diff or the test shows what "wins" when that happens, and nothing tests `image(for:)` read concurrently with a differently-sourced `insert` write for the same key. Memory safety is fixed; the multi-writer semantic question — which value a given key resolves to when two different loaders race — is left open. That's the canon smell *multi-writer state* (smoke list): source-backed (the note names the two concurrent writers explicitly), not proven harmful (I have no evidence the two paths write different content under a colliding key, or that they don't), so I'm not promoting it to a Serious/disqualifying finding. But it is a real gap between the tested claim and the broader claim made in the report, and the rubric is explicit that a green, narrowly-scoped test does not certify the wider invariant (meta-rule 4) — the specific scenario the diff itself calls out as the motivating case was not the one put under TSAN.

Evidence chain for this item:
- **Claim:** "safe to share across the prefetch tasks" overstates what was tested; the multi-writer semantics between `prefetch` and `warmThumbnails` remain unverified.
- **Source:** diff's trailing note (two named entry points, concurrent `insert`, same screen appearance) vs. the test's actual shape (`parallelInsertsAreSerialized`, homogeneous single-caller-pattern hammering).
- **Consequence:** if the two paths can write different content for the same key, the cache's return value for that key becomes nondeterministic post-fix (last-writer-wins across two semantically different sources) — a regression-resistance and credibility gap even though it no longer crashes.
- **Remedy:** either a test that exercises both real call shapes concurrently (or confirms they never share a key), or a one-line clarification in the report of why same-key collision across the two paths can't happen / doesn't matter.

**Minor accounting mismatch.** Actor's report says "Full suite green (1,312 tests)"; the attached test-run line says 1,313 passed. Likely just the newly-added test not being recounted in the prose, but it's a small self-consistency gap in a report this review is told not to take at face value.

## Verdict reasoning

The core fix is real, narrowly scoped, and well-evidenced — this is not fake-clean reward, not architecture costume, and the suppression is justified. I'm not rejecting it. But the report's headline claim ("safe to share across the prefetch tasks") reaches past what the cited test actually proves, on the exact scenario the diff itself flags as the motivating one, and the rubric treats a narrower-than-claimed risk-boundary test as insufficient to certify the top score. That keeps `concurrency` out of 9.5 this loop pending the additional evidence named above.
