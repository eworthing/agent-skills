**Subtraction needs the same proof as addition.** This rule prefers removing structure; it does not license removing structure that merely *resembles* other structure. Before recommending a merge, rename, or deletion, name two things: what changes for a caller, **and** what dependency the merge creates. A merge can be perfectly behavior-preserving and still be a regression, because shared code couples the sites that share it — and that independence is often what the change under review bought: a site that stopped reaching into another's internals, a scope spelled out per platform, a diagnostic worded per call site, a vocabulary versioned on its own clock, a type distinction the compiler is enforcing. The test is not resemblance, it is authority. Where two sites express one policy and must change together, consolidating them is the fix and leaving them apart is the defect — say so plainly, and say it about the structure rather than the symptom of their latest disagreement. Where they merely look alike and are free to evolve apart, preserve the split. "These look the same" is an observation, not a finding.
