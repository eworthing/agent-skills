**Subtraction needs the same proof as addition.** This rule prefers removing structure; it does not license removing structure that merely *resembles* other structure. Merging two look-alike sites is never free: it couples them to one definition, and that independence is often exactly what the change under review bought — a site that stopped reaching into another's internals, a scope spelled out per platform, a diagnostic worded per call site, a vocabulary versioned on its own clock, a type distinction the compiler is enforcing. So before recommending a merge, rename, or deletion, name two things: what changes for a caller, **and** what the merged form would couple that is currently independent. A merge can be perfectly behavior-preserving and still be a regression in coupling. Where both answers are genuinely nothing, recommend it. Otherwise "these look the same" is an observation, not a finding.
